# Surgery Unit Economics: Comparative Analysis (India vs. USA)

## Introduction

Surgical unit economics refers to the granular cost structure and revenue breakdown associated with performing a surgical procedure. Understanding this breakdown is vital for hospital administrators, insurers, health-tech innovators, and policymakers seeking to balance healthcare quality, access, and financial viability.

Healthcare delivery and pricing models vary dramatically across geographic borders:
- **India** primarily utilizes all-inclusive or transparent "package pricing" models influenced by private hospital networks, high out-of-pocket payments, and a growing private/public health insurance ecosystem.
- **The United States** operates on a bifurcated billing structure dividing costs into **Professional Fees** (physicians, anesthesiologists) and **Facility Fees** (operating room time, room/board, hospital equipment), dominated by complex commercial payer negotiations, chargemaster list prices, and substantial regulatory compliance overhead.

The tables below break down the mean unit economics of a major elective surgical procedure (e.g., total joint arthroplasty or major cardiothoracic procedure) in India and the United States.

## Comparative Analysis & Summary

### 1. Absolute Cost Disparity
- **~10x Nominal Cost Ratio**: The same clinical procedure that costs approximately **$4,800 (₹4,00,000)** in an accredited Indian tertiary center costs roughly **$50,000** in the United States. Even when adjusted for Purchasing Power Parity (PPP), Indian surgical care delivers substantial cost advantages.
- **Driver**: Clinical labor (nurses, technicians, residents), physical plant infrastructure, and liability insurance are vastly cheaper in India than in the US.

### 2. Shift Toward Facility Fees in the US
- In the US, **Operating Room & Facility Charges** represent the single largest expenditure (**33.0% / $16,500**), driven by high per-minute operating room charges, capital depreciation of robotic systems, and hospital facility markups.
- By contrast, India's OT charges make up **16.25%**, as fixed hospital asset costs are amortized over higher patient volumes and lower maintenance/labor costs.

### 3. Share of Implants and Consumables
- **Higher Proportion in India (31.25% vs. 22.0%)**: High-end medical devices (e.g., FDA-approved titanium joints, drug-eluting stents) are often imported from US or European manufacturers. Because device prices are globally anchored in USD or EUR, they consume nearly a third of the overall bill in India, despite government price capping on certain categories (e.g., NPPA caps on cardiac stents and knee implants).
- **Higher Absolute Dollars in the US**: While representing a smaller percentage of the total US bill (22.0%), the absolute cost ($11,000 vs. ~$1,500 equivalent) reflects substantial hospital supply-chain markups over manufacturer acquisition prices.

### 4. Surgeon & Clinician Compensation
- In India, the **Surgeon’s Fee** represents a larger share of the total bill (**12.5% vs. 9.0%**), reflecting the traditional surgeon-centric private practice model.
- In the US, while the absolute payment to the surgeon is substantially higher ($4,500 vs. ~$600 equivalent), the professional fee accounts for under 10% of the aggregate bill due to the immense scale of hospital facility fees and payer reimbursement caps.

### 5. Administrative Overhead and Structural Friction
- **US Administrative Complexity**: US hospitals allocate significant revenue to revenue cycle management (RCM), claims adjudication, prior authorizations, billing coders, and defensive medical malpractice coverage.
- **India's Lean Packaging**: Administrative expenses in India (~11.25%) are predominantly corporate hospital margin and patient registration overhead, with significantly lower administrative friction from payer disputes.
